## Surface Web, Deep Web, and Dark Web

Think of the internet as an iceberg.

**Surface Web**
The part everyone knows — websites indexed by search engines like Google, Bing, etc. Anything you can find by just searching. Examples: news sites, YouTube, Wikipedia, most company websites. This is a small fraction of the internet — often estimated around 4-5%.

**Deep Web**
Anything **not indexed** by search engines, but still perfectly normal and legal. You need a direct link, login, or specific access to reach it. Examples: your email inbox, online banking portal, private cloud storage, company internal databases, academic journal databases behind paywalls. This makes up the vast majority of the internet — most people use the deep web every day without thinking of it that way.

**Dark Web**
A small, intentionally hidden part of the deep web that requires special software to access — most commonly **Tor** (The Onion Router). It routes traffic through multiple encrypted layers/relays, making it very difficult to trace who's accessing what. Dark web sites typically use `.onion` addresses instead of normal domain names.

## What NovaPay Should Do If Employee Credentials Are Found in a Breached Database

1. **Force an immediate password reset** for every affected account — don't wait for the employee to notice.
2. **Check for reused passwords** — if that same password is used anywhere else in the company's systems, reset those too.
3. **Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)** on all affected accounts (and ideally company-wide) so a leaked password alone isn't enough to log in.
4. **Review account activity logs** for the affected accounts — check for any suspicious logins, especially from unusual locations or times (this connects directly to Event 3 in the incident).
5. **Notify the affected employees** so they're aware and can also change reused passwords on personal accounts.
6. **Investigate how the credentials were exposed** — was it this specific breach, or evidence of malware (like a keylogger) already on company devices?
7. **Consider a company-wide password policy update** — enforce strong, unique passwords and discourage reuse across services.
8. **Monitor for further exposure** — use a breach-monitoring service (like Have I Been Pwned for Business) to get alerted if more company credentials appear in future leaks.
